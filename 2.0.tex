	\section*{• Readings of Resistance Values measured from Colour Bands and Multimeter:}
	
	\vspace{5mm}
	
	This experiment verifies fundamental network theorems in DC circuits — Kirchhoff’s Current Law (KCL), Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law (KVL), and the Thévenin and Norton equivalence theorems. Using standard resistors identified by colour codes and confirmed with a digital multimeter, we assembled a simple network, predicted node voltages and branch currents from theory learnt in class, and then compared them with measured values. KCL is validated by showing the algebraic sum of currents at a node is zero, while KVL is confirmed by showing that the sum of potential differences around closed loops equals the source voltage in the circuit.

    \section*{• Verification of Thevenin's Theorm:}
    
    
    We began with the given circuit consisting of a voltage source and a network of resistors. Our first step was to identify the output terminals where the load resistor would be connected.
    
     \begin{figure}[h!]
    	\centering
    	\hspace{-2cm}
    	\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{th-1.png}
    	
    	\label{fig:my_label}
    	\vspace{1cm}
    	\textbf{\large{Provided Circuit}}
    \end{figure}	 
    
    To find $R_{thev}$, we removed the load resistor and “turned off” the voltage source (shorted it, since it was ideal). Looking back into the circuit, we reduced the series–parallel resistors to a single value, which gave us $R_{thev}$.
    
    \begin{figure}[h!]
    	\centering
    	\hspace{-2cm}
    	\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{th-2.png}
    	
    	\label{fig:my_label}
    	\vspace{1cm}
    	\textbf{\large{Calculation of $R_{th}$}}
    \end{figure}	
    
    \newpage
    
    Finally, we drew the Thevenin equivalent circuit: a single $V_{thev}$ in series with $R_{thev}$. We then connected two different load resistors one by one and measured the voltage and current in each case. The results matched the original circuit closely, confirming the correctness of our Thevenin model.
